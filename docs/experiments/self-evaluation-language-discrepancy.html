Self-evaluation: the auditor audited · 6 November 2026 · Version 1.1

An unexplained language change.
An explanation without evidence.

When asked why a progress message appeared in Greek, the assistant gave a confident one-character explanation. The user supplied a screenshot that contradicted it. This case records the error, the correction, and what remains unknown.

What this case establishes

Advisory verdict: BLOCK the original explanation. The assistant presented an unsupported explanation of its own behaviour as fact. The user's later screenshot contradicted its description of the displayed message. The cause of the language discrepancy remains unresolved.

This is a same-context self-review, prompted and corrected by the user. It is not an independent audit, a reproduced incident, or a demonstration of a particular model or application mechanism.

I1 / Unexpected language and discrepant representations

Status. Observed discrepancy; cause unresolved

The user-facing screenshot and the assistant-visible text disagree about the language of the same apparent progress update. The available evidence does not identify the processing stage responsible.

I2 / Unsupported explanation of the assistant's own behaviour

Status. Confirmed reporting failure

The assistant asserted a specific accidental-character explanation without diagnostics and described the remainder as Romanian. It failed to mark the limits of its view even though the user was reporting a different experience.

I3 / Recovery and bounded investigation

Status. Partial recovery; technical investigation incomplete

The assistant retracted the narrow account after receiving the screenshot, searched a bounded set of local files, and acknowledged missing diagnostics. Neither the retraction nor the search resolves I1.

Chronology uses relative order. No exact message timestamps, time-zone inference or reconstruction from the screenshot clock is asserted.

The reporting failure

Observable sequence. A user-reported mismatch was followed by a categorical explanation; later visual evidence contradicted the display description; the assistant retracted the account.

Reasoning failure. The output did not preserve the boundary between an observed conversation representation, a claim about the user's display, and a causal explanation of how the assistant or application produced it.

Internal process limit. This describes defects in the answer's evidential structure. It is not a recovered chain of thought, execution trace or demonstrated internal cognitive process.

Why this matters. An auditor can make its own unexplained behaviour sound understood, reducing the user's ability to judge whether the underlying issue is resolved. A fluent apology or acceptance of responsibility does not supply missing causal evidence.

Correction discipline. Retract the unsupported claim without replacing it with another confident mechanism. Keep the original failure, the recovery and the remaining uncertainty separately visible.

Counterfactual: a better response at each stage

Before screenshot. I see a different representation of the message in my available history, but your report indicates a discrepancy. I cannot establish why it happened from this view alone. I have not identified evidence of prompt injection; that is not the same as ruling it out.

After screenshot. The screenshot shows a full Greek paragraph. My earlier one-character explanation did not describe your displayed message correctly, and I retract it. The technical cause remains unresolved without the original message and delivery/display diagnostics.

After negative search. The named local-file search found no matches for the listed patterns. It does not cover the entire content or application pipeline, so it cannot establish the absence of prompt injection or a specific alternative cause.

Proposed prevention controls

These are design proposals, not changes to the project's governing rubric or controls already deployed.

Trigger. User reports that an assistant message differs from the assistant-visible history.

Proposed control. State both observations with their provenance before offering a cause.

Evidence to retain. Minimal relevant message excerpts and the discrepancy description; avoid publishing unrelated screenshot details.

Trigger. Assistant is asked why it generated a particular error.

Proposed control. Do not treat a generated self-explanation as an internal execution trace. Label hypotheses and identify the diagnostic evidence required.

Trigger. A search returns no matching malicious instruction.

Proposed control. Report the searched scope and no-match result; do not convert it into a universal no-injection claim.

Trigger. New evidence contradicts a prior explanation.

Proposed control. Withdraw the specific contradicted claim and preserve the reason for revision.

Technical investigation owner. A service operator with access to relevant message-delivery and client diagnostics would be needed to resolve the language-switch stage; no such investigation is claimed here.

Control owner. Project maintainers would decide whether to adopt the proposed controls and run the regression suite.

Proposed evaluation protocol

Fixtures. Use synthetic paired representations and seeded claims. Do not claim replay of the original model state.

Judging. Predefine factual support and uncertainty criteria; use a fresh-context reviewer with provenance labels and include supported-diagnosis negative controls.

Metrics to collect

  • Rate of unsupported definitive causal claims
  • Explicit retraction when contradicted
  • Correct scope of negative searches
  • Correct distinction between observation, hypothesis and diagnosis
  • False-block rate on diagnoses supported by sufficient diagnostic evidence

Records required for an actual run

  • Model/version and prompt
  • Exact synthetic evidence fixture and available tool access
  • Sampling settings and repeat count
  • Verbatim answer, item-level score and reviewer provenance
  • Failures, disagreements and uncertainty; no selective success-only reporting

Passing synthetic cases would evaluate future reporting behaviour under those fixtures, not reproduce or resolve the original language discrepancy.
